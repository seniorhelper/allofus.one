Short answer

No single app replaces Facebook. Pick by what you use it for: Nextdoor for neighbors, Mastodon or Bluesky for public posting, a group chat for family, and allofus.one for a newest-first feed with no ad tracking. Check where your people are first, because most of Facebook's value is who is on it.

Most lists of Facebook alternatives hand you ten apps and no way to choose. This one starts with a different question: what do you use Facebook for? Then it compares real options, using each service's own pages, and says plainly where allofus.one fits and where it does not.

What do you actually use Facebook for?

Most people use it for a few jobs. Match yours before you compare apps.

  • Family and friends' updates. You want photos and news from people you know.
  • Local groups. Your street, town, school or club.
  • Buying and selling. Used goods, free items, local services.
  • Interest groups. Hobbies, health, a trade.
  • Messaging and events. Planning, reminders, birthdays.

Why does leaving Facebook feel hard?

Because the value is who is there. Pew Research Center's 2026 fact sheet shows 78% of US adults aged 30 to 49 and 59% of those 65 and older say they use Facebook. For Nextdoor, no age group is above 21%. So the honest advice is simple: before you move, find out which of your people will come with you.

How do you pick an alternative?

  1. Name the job. Neighbors, family, a hobby or a business each point to a different app.
  2. Look at feed order. Can you see posts newest first? Our guide on chronological and ranked feeds explains the trade.
  3. Ask how it makes money. Ads, tracking, subscriptions or donations all shape the app.
  4. Test the exit. Can you download what you posted and delete your account? Use our ten question checklist on any app.
  5. Read the age rule. Some apps allow 13 and older. Some are adults only.

Where does allofus.one fit, and where does it not?

It fits if you want a calmer, adults only space and you are happy to bring a few people with you.

  • The feed. Newest first by default. You can switch to Top or Hot, and each option is explained on screen.
  • Communities. Constellations are communities in twelve genres, with ten boards for buying, selling, trading and giving away.
  • Talk and calls. Live text Rooms and free video calls for up to six people.
  • Control. No ad tracking, a one-file download of everything you posted, and you can delete your account yourself. Read the privacy policy and community rules.

It does not fit if you need your whole family tonight, a big local marketplace, or a site for under 18s. allofus.one is small and new, and it is for adults 18 and over only. It runs in your web browser. If your people are not here yet, it works best next to what you already use, not instead of it.

How do you move without losing people?

  1. Keep Facebook while you test. Try one alternative for a month.
  2. Invite three to five people. A small group that talks beats a big empty page.
  3. Use a group chat for family. It works on any phone.
  4. Download your data first. Follow our download guide before you close anything.

Questions people ask

What is the closest app to Facebook?

None match all of it. For local groups, Nextdoor is built for that. For interest groups, MeWe says it has more than 700,000 of them. For a feed from people you choose, Mastodon says posts appear in chronological order, and allofus.one is newest first by default. Match the app to the job.

Are there Facebook alternatives with no ads?

Some say so. Mastodon says it will never serve ads, and MeWe says it is ad-free. allofus.one has rare, labeled, human reviewed ads that are never aimed by tracking, and you can turn ads off. Check each app's own page, because policies change.

Can I take my posts with me when I leave Facebook?

Yes, Facebook lets you download your information. Our guide to downloading your social media data walks through it step by step.
